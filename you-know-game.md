# You Know ____ Is a Lot Like ____

## Game Idea

`You Know ____ Is a Lot Like ____` is a fast, funny improv-style game for Microsoft CSA teams. Each round, a player has to connect a real technical activity that a CSA might do with a completely unrelated everyday role, event, or situation.

The fun comes from making a convincing comparison between two things that should not logically go together.

Example:

`You know designing a landing zone is a lot like hosting Thanksgiving dinner.`

Then the player has to explain why.

## Why It Works

- It rewards technical understanding without turning into trivia.
- It makes people explain complex work in plain language.
- It creates good team humor without needing inside jokes that exclude people.
- It works as an icebreaker, team meeting game, or workshop warm-up.

## Objective

Score points by making the most creative, accurate, and entertaining comparison between:

- a CSA technical task
- a random everyday role, event, or life situation

The winner is the player or team with the most points after a set number of rounds.

## Players

- 3 to 12 players works best
- Can be played individually or in teams

## What You Need

- A timer
- A way to track score
- Two prompt lists or two decks of cards

## Prompt Structure

Every round uses two blanks:

- First blank: a technical thing a CSA at Microsoft does
- Second blank: a random non-sequitur everyday life thing, job, or situation

### Examples For Blank One

- designing a landing zone
- reviewing an architecture
- planning a migration
- troubleshooting identity issues
- writing a post-incident summary
- tuning cost optimization
- mapping dependencies
- setting up governance controls
- preparing for a customer workshop

### Examples For Blank Two

- being a wedding planner
- running a food truck
- coaching a soccer team
- packing for a family vacation
- hosting a dinner party
- working as an air traffic controller
- organizing a garage sale
- taking care of a zoo
- managing a school field trip

## How To Play

1. One player draws or is assigned one item from each list.
2. They read the full statement out loud: `You know [technical thing] is a lot like [random thing].`
3. They get 30 seconds to think.
4. They have 60 to 90 seconds to explain the comparison.
5. The group or a judge scores the answer.

## Scoring

Use a simple 1 to 5 scale in three categories:

- Accuracy: Does the technical analogy actually make sense?
- Creativity: Was the connection unexpected and clever?
- Delivery: Was it clear, funny, or memorable?

Maximum score per round: 15 points.

## Suggested Round Flow

### Standard Round

- One player presents
- Everyone else scores
- Highest cumulative score wins after 5 rounds

### Battle Round

- Two players get the same prompt pair
- Each gives their explanation
- The room votes for the better comparison

### Team Round

- Small teams get 2 minutes to prepare together
- One spokesperson delivers the final analogy

## Rules Of A Good Answer

A strong answer should do at least two of these well:

- explain the process in plain English
- match steps or roles from one side to the other
- show risks, tradeoffs, or coordination points
- land on a memorable image people can repeat later

## Example Round

Prompt:

`You know troubleshooting identity issues is a lot like being a nightclub bouncer.`

Possible answer:

"Both jobs are really about figuring out who should be allowed in, who is blocked for a reason, and whether the problem is the person, the list, or the rules at the door. If the identity system is wrong, the right people get denied. If the policy is too loose, the wrong people get in. And when there is pressure from outside, you still have to stick to the rules without slowing everything down."

## Winning Conditions

Choose one:

- First player to 30 points wins
- Highest score after everyone has played 3 times wins
- Best of 5 battle rounds wins

## Best Use Cases

- Team offsites
- Workshop warm-ups
- CSA community calls
- New team onboarding
- End-of-week team meetings

## Optional Variations

### Lightning Mode

- 15 seconds to think
- 30 seconds to explain

### Brutal Mode

- The audience can ask one follow-up question
- The player has to defend the analogy

### Customer-Friendly Mode

- No internal acronyms allowed
- Extra point if the explanation would make sense to a customer executive

## Short Pitch

This is a game about proving you understand technical work well enough to make it funny, simple, and unforgettable.
